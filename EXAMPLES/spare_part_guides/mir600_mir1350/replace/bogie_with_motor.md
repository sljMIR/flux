Failure to use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) can result in serious injury.

Determine relevant and necessary PPE based on the task, environment, and experience of personnel. The following is a list of common PPE you must consider:

*   Safety shoes to protect from impact or puncture hazards.
    
*   Safety glasses and face shield to protect from flying debris or chemical agents.
    
*   Hard hat to protect from falling or flying objects.
    
*   Gloves to protect against sharp objects, electrical hazards, chemical agents, and extreme temperatures.
    
*   Ear plugs or earmuffs to protect from high noise hazards.
    
*   Dust mask or respirator to protect from inhaling chemical agents or other harmful airborne hazards.

Electrical components can be damaged by electrostatic discharge. Consider moving the robot to an ESD protected area and using ESD protected clothing and tools.

The spare part for this robot is heavy. Before performing the replacement, make the necessary arrangements to ensure a safe work environment.

The bogie and motor are provided by MiR only as one combined package.

## Things you will need

*   A new bogie with a motor from MiR
    
*   A torque wrench spanning 11.6–90 Nm
    
*   A 22 mm hex socket  
    
*   A T30 screwdriver
    
*   A flat-head screwdriver
    
*   A pair of slip-joint pliers
    
*   A support block to raise the robot
    
*   Contact cleaning spray
    
*   Universal degreaser spray
    
*   An air compressor

## Before replacing the motor

1.  Note down the total distance the robot has driven. You can find this in the robot interface on the default dashboard under Moved. MiR may ask you for this information if we request the motor for analysis.
    
2.  Turn off the robot and disconnect the battery in the left side compartment. To disconnect the battery, pull out the Battery lock pin, and pull the Battery lever downwards until the Battery lock pin is in the middle position.  
    
    <image: "Disconnect.png">
    
3.  On the side with the bogie you want to replace, press the two buttons on the hatch.  
    
    <image: "access_side_compartment_1.png">
    
4.  Pull open the hatch.
    
    <image: "access_side_compartment_2.png">
    
5.  Disconnect the status light cable.
    
    <image: "access_side_compartment_3.png">
    
6.  Turn the two hinges in the bottom corners to release the hatch, and remove the hatch from the robot.  
    
    <image: "access_side_compartment_4.png">

## Accessing the front compartment

1.  Turn the two screws on the front compartment hatch 90˚. Use a flat-head screwdriver.  
    
    <image: "access_front_compartment_1.png">
    
2.  Pull out the electronics drawer.
    
    <image: "access_front_compartment_2.png">

## Removing the corner cover

1.  Press the Emergency stop button on the corner cover you are going to remove. After reassembling, remember to release the button again.
    
2.  Remove the four screws from the corner cover using a T30 screwdriver. When reattaching the corner covers, tighten the screws to 11.6 Nm.  
    
    <image: "access_corner_shield_1.png">
    
3.  Remove the corner cover. It may require some force to disconnect the shield from the corner bumper. Pull it downward firmly to remove it from the bumper.
    
    <image: "access_corner_shield_3.png">

## Pulling out the bogie arm

1.  Raise the robot above ground, and use a piece of wood or other firm material to keep it lifted—see the section _Transportation_ in the manual for instructions on how to lift the robot safely. The drive wheel must not be in contact with the floor.
    
    <image: "bogie_lift_robot.png">
    
2.  Remove the bogie screw. Use a 22 mm hex socket. When fastening the new bogie, tighten the screw to 90 Nm.
    
    <image: "bogie_unscrew_bogie_bolt.png">
    
3.  Pull out the wheel and bogie arm slightly. Pull out the bogie arm enough to make it possible to gain access behind the wheel gearing.  
    
    <image: "bogie_pull_out_bogie.png">
    
4.  Make sure the bogie is stable and supported. You can use support blocks or ask another person to help hold it.
    
    <image: "bogie_Support_bogie.png">
    
    Be careful not to pull any wires while pulling out the bogie arm. If necessary, unplug any wires that are not long enough to let you pull out the bogie far enough for you to reach behind it.
    
5.  Disconnect the following three cables located behind the bogie beneath the center compartment. There is an identical set of cables on the other side. Only disconnect the three cables closest to you.
    
    Label the cables clearly. When reattaching these cables, make sure that each cable is attached to the equivalent sockets on the replacement bogie with motor. If the cables are switched, it can damage the robot.
    
    To disconnect the brake power cable you may need to use a pair of slip-joint pliers to loosen the connector lock. The brake power cable is the cable that is closest to the motor.
    
    <image: "bogie_Cables_2.png">
    
6.  Disconnect the motor power cable and the hall sensor cable directly from the motor. The bogie arm with the motor is now detached.
    
    <image: "bogie_cables3.png">
    
7.  Pull the bogie free of the robot completely. You have now removed the bogie arm and can replace it with a new one.
    
    <image: "bogie_Remove_bogie.png">

## Replacing the bogie

To install the new bogie into the robot, complete all of the disassembly steps in the reverse order with the new bogie. When fastening the bogie screw, tighten the screw to 90 Nm. When reattaching the corner covers, tighten the screws to 11.6 Nm.

When reattaching the cables, make sure that each cable is attached to the correct sockets on the replacement bogie with motor. If the cables are switched, it can damage the robot.

Make sure that there is a gap of at least 2 mm between the drive wheel and the side cover. Otherwise, the wheel might scrape against the cover when driving.

There are two cable clips on the back of the gearbox. Fix the motor power cable inside these clips to keep the cable from dragging against the ground. After reassembling, make sure that the motor cables are not dragging against the ground. If so, rearrange the cables in the cable clips to make sure they stay lifted.

<image: "cable_clips.png">

Make sure there is no dust on the motor, gearbox, plate, or the driveshaft metal bracket when assembling.
