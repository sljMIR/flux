Failure to use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) can result in serious injury.

Determine relevant and necessary PPE based on the task, environment, and experience of personnel. The following is a list of common PPE you must consider:

*   Safety shoes to protect from impact or puncture hazards.
    
*   Safety glasses and face shield to protect from flying debris or chemical agents.
    
*   Hard hat to protect from falling or flying objects.
    
*   Gloves to protect against sharp objects, electrical hazards, chemical agents, and extreme temperatures.
    
*   Ear plugs or earmuffs to protect from high noise hazards.
    
*   Dust mask or respirator to protect from inhaling chemical agents or other harmful airborne hazards.

Electrical components can be damaged by electrostatic discharge. Consider moving the robot to an ESD protected area and using ESD protected clothing and tools.

This guide describes how to replace the power board on a MiR600 or MiR1350 robot. The power board is responsible for powering many of the electric components in the robot. It is located in the electronics drawer in the front compartment.

If possible, use an ESD workstation when replacing the power board to ensure none of the electric circuits are damaged.

## Things you will need

*   A 2.6 Nm torque wrench with a T25 head.
    
*   A flat-head screwdriver

## Before replacing the power board

Turn off the robot, and disconnect the battery in the left side compartment. To disconnect the battery, pull out the Battery lock pin, and pull the Battery lever downwards until the Battery lock pin is in the middle position.

<image: "Disconnect_v2.png">

Turn the two screws on the front compartment hatch 90˚, and pull out the electronics drawer.

<image: "access_front_compartment_1.png"> <image: "access_front_compartment_2.png">

## Replacing the power board

**Take several pictures of the power board so you can see where each cable is connected.** Although this guide provides a connector overview of where each cable is connected—see [](#Reconnec)—, it is a good idea to have an image of the original setup as a cross reference in case a cable is missing a label.

**Disconnect all of the connected cables.** Make sure to pull the connectors out straight to avoid bending any pins.

<image: "0016.png">

**Loosen the two screws at the top of the power board using a T25 screwdriver.**

<image: "0017.png">

**Pull the power board up making sure to slide it out of the spring mount.**

<image: "0018.png"> <image: "0019.png">

**Remove the two screws completely from the power board and screw them loosely into the new power board in the same positions.**

<image: "0021.png">

**Slide the new power board into place making sure that is slides under the spring mount and the two screws slide into their positions at the top of the drawer.**

<image: "0020.png">

**Tighten the two screws at the top of the power board to 2.6 Nm.**

<image: "0017_2.png">

**Reconnect the cables to the power board.** The cables are labeled according to which socket they must be connected to.

<image: "powerboard_overview.png">

**Reassemble the robot by following the step in** [Before replacing the power board](#Before) **in reverse.**
