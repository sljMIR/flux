# How to replace the thermal switch on MiR600 and MiR1350

Failure to use appropriate personal protective equipment (PPE) can result in serious injury.

Determine relevant and necessary PPE based on the task, environment, and experience of personnel. The following is a list of common PPE you must consider:

*   Safety shoes to protect from impact or puncture hazards.
    
*   Safety glasses and face shield to protect from flying debris or chemical agents.
    
*   Hard hat to protect from falling or flying objects.
    
*   Gloves to protect against sharp objects, electrical hazards, chemical agents, and extreme temperatures.
    
*   Ear plugs or earmuffs to protect from high noise hazards.
    
*   Dust mask or respirator to protect from inhaling chemical agents or other harmful airborne hazards.

Electrical components can be damaged by electrostatic discharge. Consider moving the robot to an ESD protected area and using ESD protected clothing and tools.

This guide describes how to replace the thermal switch, the SICK stack bracket, and the SICK stack bottom plate. The new SICK stack bracket will allow the replacement thermal switch to be relocated to a more optimal position. The new SICK stack bottom plate will improve heat dissipation around the SICK stack.

## Things you will need

*   A T10 bit
    
*   A T20 bit
    
*   A T30 bit
    
*   Wire cutters (for zip ties)
    
*   Zip ties for loose wires
    
*   A pair of pliers
    
*   A lift and support blocks to keep the robot stabilized as you need to access the bottom of the front drawer.

## Kit contents

*   Thermal switch (505688)
    
*   Thermal switch mount
        
*   Extension cord for connecting the new thermal switch to the existing relay wires.
        
*   Sticky foam pad for secure attachment between thermal switch mount and the vertical metal bracket in the front compartment.
        
*   SICK stack bracket (602007)
    
*   SICK stack bottom plate (600841)

## Before replacing the thermal switch

1.  Turn off the robot, and disconnect the battery in the left side compartment. To disconnect the battery, pull out the Battery lock pin, and pull the Battery lever downwards until the Battery lock pin is in the middle position.
    
    <image: "Disconnect.png">
    
2.  Lift the robot onto a secure structure where you can safely access the area under the front compartment. To ensure the robot is not damaged during the lifting, see the manual for your robot application for lifting specifications.
    
    If you are under the robot while it is lifted, you risk serious injury if the robot falls or slips.
    
*   Stand clear of the robot while it is raised in the air.
        
*   Only approach the robot when it is stationed on a secure structure.
        
*   See the section about lifting the robot in the manual for your robot application.  

        You can find this guide on MiR Support Portal.

3.  If your robot is equipped with a top module that extends over the front of the robot, you may need to remove it in order to have enough space to perform the thermal switch replacement.

## Remove the old thermal switch and SICK stack bracket

1.  Remove the two screws holding the thermal switch. Use a T10 bit. Undo the relay wires from the switch.
    
    <image: "Remove_thermal_switch.png">
    
2.  Remove the screws holding the SICK stack bracket in place. Use a T30 bit.
    
    <image: "Remove_screws_SICK_stack_bracket.png">
    
3.  Slide off the covers for the three harness tube clamps.
    
    <image: "Remove_harness_clamps.png">
    
4.  Remove the harness tubes from the clamps.
    
    <image: "Remove_harness_wires.png">
    
5.  Remove the three harness tube clamps. Use a T30 bit.
    
    <image: "Remove_screws_below_harness_clamps.png">
    
6.  Cut the zip ties along the SICK stack bracket in the center and along the left side arm.
    
7.  The SICK stack bracket can now be removed from the front compartment.
    
    You may need to disconnect additional cables for easier access to remove the bracket, but this is optional. The SICK stack bracket can be removed without disconnecting any wires to the SICK stack.
    
    <image: "Remove_SICK_bracket.png">
    
8.  Install the new SICK stack bracket reusing the same screws and screw holes, including the screws for the harness tube clamps.
    
9.  Refit the three harness tubes in the harness tube clamps, and reinstall the covers.

## Remove the old SICK stack bottom plate

1.  Unscrew the three screws in the SICK stack bottom plate on the underside of the front compartment. Use a T20 bit. Note that only the top left screw needs to be fully removed for the plate to be removed, the other two screws can be left untightened.
    
    <image: "Remove_SICK_bottom_plate.png">
    
2.  Install the new SICK stack bottom plate, and retighten the screws. Note that the direction of the two slots that grab the two screws to one side are mirrored on the new bottom plate.

## Install the new thermal switch

1.  Bend the small fingers of the thermal switch mount upwards 90 degrees. Use a pair of pliers.
    
    <image: "Bend_fingers_thermal_switch_bracket.png">
    
2.  Attach the red ends of the extension cord to the new thermal switch.
    
    <image: "Connect_extension_chords_to_thermal_switch_light.png">
    
3.  Line the holes in the thermal switch up with the small fingers and bend them down again until the thermal switch sits securely.
    
    <image: "Mount_thermal_switch_on_bracket.png">
    
4.  Peel off the paper on one side of the sticky foam pad.
    
    <image: "Peel_off_paper_from_sticky_pad_light.png">
    
5.  Attach the sticky foam pad on the back of the thermal switch mount.
    
    <image: "Mount_sticky_pad_backside.png">
    
6.  Peel off the paper from the other side of the sticky foam pad. Press the thermal switch mount against the vertical metal bracket on the left side of the front compartment. The long forks of the thermal switch mount should be pointing downwards.
    
    <image: "Mount_new_thermal_switch.png">
    
7.  Bend the four large fingers of the thermal switch mount around the bracket so they hug it. Use a pair of pliers.
    
    <image: "Bend_fingers_to_hug_post.png">
    
8.  Connect the extension cables of the new thermal switch to the wires from the robot. Note the labels on the wires so that "IN" is connected to "IN" and "OUT" is connected to "OUT".
    
    <image: "Connect_old_wires_to_extension_cord.png">
    
9.  Reattach any removed wires and make sure that all screws are securely tightened.
    
10.  Secure any loose wires to the new SICK stack bracket. The plastic cable clips can be reused. Alternatively, use zip ties to secure loose wires.

## Troubleshooting

*   Check if there are any loose wires or connections.
    
*   Check that the thermal switch extension cord is properly connected to the relay wires coming from the robot. Any exposed connectors that touches metal could result in a short circuit.
